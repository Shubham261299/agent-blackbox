# A ten-minute test with people outside tech

The automatic checks (no jargon on any page, a reading grade of about 4, every number from the stored results) say the demo *should* be easy to follow. Only a real person can say whether it is. This test is for you to run with **two or three people who do not work in tech**: a friend, a relative, someone in HR or sales. Ten minutes each.

## Before you start

- Open the live demo on their screen or phone: https://shubham261299.github.io/agent-blackbox/
- Say only this: *"I would like to see how a stranger reads this page. There are no wrong answers, and I will not help."*
- **Do not explain anything while they read.** If they ask "what does this mean?", write down the exact words they asked about, and say "what do you think it means?".
- Write down their answers in their own words, not yours.

## Test 1: five seconds

Show the front page for five seconds, then cover it. Ask:

1. What is this about?
2. Who do you think it is for?

## Test 2: one minute

Let them read the front page for one minute, with no scrolling help. Then ask, one at a time:

1. In your own words, what did the person build?
2. Was there something that worked? What?
3. Was there something that did not work? What?
4. Is there anything you did not understand, or a word you tripped on?

## Test 3: one task

Say: *"One of these runs failed. Can you find which step went wrong?"* Let them start from the front page, or press "3 minutes". Note whether they:

- found the failed run without help,
- noticed the step marked as failed,
- noticed "Where to look first".

## What counts as passing

- At least two of three can say, in their own words, that it **records what an AI agent does and checks it** (Test 1 or 2.1).
- At least two of three can say **one thing worked and one did not** (Test 2.2 and 2.3). The exact numbers do not matter.
- At least two of three **find the failed step** (Test 3).

If someone stumbles, that is the finding. Send me the table, and in particular the **words they tripped on**: I change those words and the checks that guard them, and we try the changed page on the next person.

## What this test cannot tell you

Two or three people is a sample too small for any percentage. It finds confusing words and missing explanations; it does not measure how many visitors will understand.
